Lesson 05 · Projects · read in ~6 minutes

A room
for the work

A project is a self-contained workspace with its own instructions, its own knowledge and its own chats. What it really removes is the first five minutes of every conversation — the re-uploading, the re-explaining, the reminding Claude who you are and how you write.

Part 01

What's inside one

Three compartments, and the arrows are the whole point: every chat you start inside the project already has the instructions and the knowledge loaded. You never set them up twice.

Instructions → how Claude behaves
↓ both inherited ↓
Knowledge → what Claude knows

Compartment 01

Instructions

Standing orders for this workspace: tone, expertise level, response style, process, non-negotiables. They apply to every conversation here and work alongside your global preferences and styles.

Compartment 02

Knowledge

Documents Claude references across all chats in the project. Upload once. No more attaching the same brand guide to the eleventh conversation about the same campaign.

Compartment 03

Chats

Where the work happens. Every conversation started inside the project automatically has the knowledge base available and follows the instructions.

Worth getting straight early

Chats in a project don't read each other. What's shared is the instructions and the knowledge base — not the contents of your other conversations. If something from Tuesday's chat needs to persist, it has to go into the knowledge base or the instructions. This trips people up because "self-contained workspace" sounds like everything inside can see everything else.

Reference material you reuseMeeting notes, survey results, reports, historical data — anything you'd otherwise re-attach.
Consistent requirementsAlways formal. Always cite sources. Always follow our template.
People who need the same footingSeveral of you working from one shared foundation rather than five private versions of it.

The test: is this ongoing, or a one-off? One question doesn't need a room. A work stream does.

Part 02

Building one

Three steps, genuinely in order — the setup, then the instructions, then the files. Ten minutes, once.

Step 01

Set it up

  • Hover the left sidebar and click Projects, or go straight to claude.ai/projects.
  • Click + New Project in the upper right.
  • Give it a descriptive name — "Q4 Marketing Campaign", "Product Documentation".
  • Add a short description. Claude doesn't read this — it's there so you and your teammates know what the room is for.
  • Choose visibility: private, or shared with your organisation.
Step 02

Write the instructions

  • Click Instructions to open the panel, then Save instructions when you're done. They apply to every chat in the project from then on.
Context — what this is"This project is for creating marketing content for our B2B software product."
Process — the order of work"First consider a blog structure that will entice this audience, then write the draft."
Tone & style"Use a professional but conversational tone. Avoid jargon when possible."
Requirements — the non-negotiables"Always include a call-to-action at the end of marketing copy."
The move most people never try: instructions can automate a workflow, not just set a tone. "When I upload a meeting transcript, create a structured summary using this template." Now the project does something the moment you drop a file in it.

Look at those four ingredients and you'll recognise them: context, task, rules. Project instructions are Lesson 02's prompt framework, written once instead of every time.

Back to the three moves
Step 03

Build the knowledge base

  • The files menu sits on the right of the project's main page. Click + to add.
  • Formats include PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML and more — or connect Google Drive to link documents directly.
  • Reference documents: brand guidelines, style guides, templates.
  • Background material: research reports, meeting notes, requirements docs.
  • Examples of work you want Claude to emulate — the fastest way to communicate a standard.
  • Technical documentation and specs.
Name files like you mean it. Claude uses filenames to find the right document, so Q4-2024-Brand-Guidelines.pdf beats document1.pdf. This matters more than it sounds — see the next section for why.
Part 03

What happens when it gets big

Projects change how they work as the knowledge base grows. You don't configure anything and you won't be asked — but knowing the switch exists explains a lot about how to organise your files.

Under the limit

Everything loaded

Your whole knowledge base sits in context. Claude has all of it in view at once, every chat.

↓ knowledge grows ↓
the switch flips itself
At the limit and beyond

Searched, not loaded

Claude stops loading everything and instead searches your project files, pulling in only the parts relevant to your question. This is called retrieval augmented generation, or RAG.

10×

Roughly how much more your project can hold once it switches to searching — with response quality held steady. You'll see an indicator when it's on, but the experience feels the same.

Why this changes your habits: once Claude is searching rather than reading everything, the filename is the search handle. Descriptive names and related files grouped together stop being tidiness and start being retrieval.

The plan detail the lesson leaves out

This automatic scaling is a paid-plan feature (Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise). On a free account, projects still work and you can create a handful of them — but keep the knowledge base lean, because nothing will step in to manage the overflow for you.

Part 04

Sharing it

Claude for Work · Team & Enterprise

Three permission levels, and each one contains the one below it. Pick the lowest rung that lets someone do their job.

Rung 01

Can view

See the project, use the knowledge, and chat inside it — but change nothing. Read-only, with full discussion rights.

Rung 02

Can edit

Everything above, plus modify the instructions, update the knowledge base, and manage other members. Full collaboration.

Rung 03

Owner

Everything above, plus control of who sees the project at all — specific people, or the whole organisation.

How to actually share it
  1. Open the project and click Share project, to the right of the project name.
  2. Add people by name or email — or paste a list of addresses for bulk sharing. It'll appear in their Shared with you.
  3. Or share with Everyone at [your organisation] to make it discoverable in the Team tab.
  4. People get an email notification, and find shared projects under Shared with me.
Part 05

Five to steal

Not sure where to start? Copy one of these outright. The pattern is the same each time — load the material, set the standing rules, then work.

Q4 product launch

What goes in

Product specs, competitive analysis, messaging brainstorm notes. Claude then has all of it in mind for any question or draft you throw at it.

Research support

What goes in

Competitive review, user research data, customer feedback. Use it to synthesise sources, draft reports, and keep recommendations consistent with each other.

Client account hub

What goes in

The client's brand guidelines, past deliverables, communication history — plus instructions so Claude matches their tone on every proposal and report.

Event planning workspace

What goes in

Venue contracts, speaker bios, attendee data. Out come run-of-show documents, attendee comms and post-event reports that stay on theme.

Job description generator

What goes in

Past job descriptions, team charters, internal headcount requests. Drafts then reflect how your team actually works, not a generic template.
