Lesson 08 · Connectors · read in ~6 minutes

Somewhere
to point

A connector doesn't make Claude smarter. It gives your request somewhere to reach — your inbox, your docs, your team's channel. Instead of starting every conversation from scratch, you can ask for things that live outside the message you typed.

Try it — this one actually works

Same request. Different reach.

Below is a request you might send. Switch a source off and watch the clause that depended on it lose its footing. Nothing about the request changes — only what it's able to point at.

Your request to Claude Draft a short status update on the budget project for my manager.No source Note the two questions finance raised over email last week, and how we're answering them.Email Match the tone the team has been using in the project channel this week.Team chat

Three sentences. The update, the finance answers, in your team's voice. Every clause found something to reach.

Two sentences. Right voice, but the finance answers dropped out — that thread is sitting in an inbox Claude can't open.

Two sentences. The facts are there, written in a default voice, because the conversation that carries your team's tone was never in the room.

One sentence. A generic status update. Everything you asked for beyond your own words had nowhere to go.

Cloud storage is on — and nothing changed. Nothing in this request points at your files, so the connection sits idle. A connector only earns its place when a request reaches for it.

The point, in one line: Claude didn't go looking on its own. You wrote every clause — each connection only added somewhere a clause could land. Turn a source off and that part of the request has nowhere to go, so it comes back out.

One port, many tools

Why they all fit

MCP is USB-C for AI.

The Model Context Protocol is one universal standard, so Claude talks to hundreds of different applications through a single consistent interface. Because it's open, anyone can build a connector for any tool and it works with Claude without special-casing. That's the whole reason the directory can be enormous rather than a short list of partnerships.

Type 01

Web connectors

Link Claude to cloud services — Gmail, Google Drive, Notion, Slack, Asana, Linear, Stripe and many more. They run remotely, so they work across your devices.

Type 02

Desktop extensions

Run locally on your computer through the Claude Desktop app, reaching things a cloud service can't.

  • Local file access for reading and organising documents
  • Browser control for automated web tasks
  • Native app integration, like Figma for design work
Part 02

Connecting one

Anthropic keeps a directory at claude.ai/directory. You can also reach it from any chat: click the + button near the message box, then choose Connectors.

01
Find itBrowse the directory, or use + → Connectors in any chat.
02
Click ConnectSelect the connector you want to add.
03
AuthenticateYou're sent to the service's own login page. Sign in with the credentials you already have — Claude never sees your password.
04
Grant permissionsRead what's actually being requested before you authorise. This screen is the one people click through fastest and should read slowest.
05
Test itBack in Claude, try something small: "Can you access my [tool name]?" Confirm it works before you rely on it.
If you need a desktop extension

Four steps, one app

  • Download and install the Claude Desktop app — the web interface can't do local access.
  • Open it and go to Settings → Extensions.
  • Browse the available extensions and click Install.
  • Follow any extra setup specific to that extension.
The directory label your source doesn't mention

Look for "verified" before you connect

Course material tends to describe the directory as two tabs — web and desktop. That split is real, but it isn't the label that matters for safety. Anthropic's current documentation describes the directory as holding verified and community connectors.

  • Verified connectors have been reviewed by Anthropic for security, reliability and compatibility.
  • Community connectors pass automated checks but are not reviewed in depth by Anthropic.
  • The label describes how much scrutiny it has had, not how it behaves — both work identically once connected. Which is exactly why the label is the thing to read.
  • On a Team plan, if you can't enable connectors yourself you'll see a Request button that sends it to your admins instead.

Source: Anthropic connector documentation, checked August 2026. The same catalogue serves Claude.ai, Desktop, mobile, Cowork and Claude Code.

Part 03

What you can now ask

Once a tool is connected, Claude takes it into account when answering. These are the shapes of request that only become possible on the other side of a connection.

Project management

Asana · Linear · Jira
  • "What are my highest priority tasks due this week?"
  • "Create a new task for reviewing the Q4 budget proposal."
  • "Summarise the status of our product launch project."

Communication

Slack · Gmail
  • "Find the email thread where we discussed the vendor contract."
  • "Draft a reply to the latest message in the #marketing channel."
  • "What did the team decide about the timeline in yesterday's discussion?"

Documentation

Notion · Google Drive · Confluence
  • "Search our documentation for our brand voice guidelines."
  • "Summarise the meeting notes from last week's product review."
  • "What does our style guide say about using contractions?"

Business tools

Stripe · PayPal · Salesforce
  • "Show me revenue trends for the past quarter."
  • "What's the status of the Acme Corp opportunity?"
  • "List recent transactions over $1,000."

Read those again as a pattern: every one of them names a thing that lives somewhere else. That's the tell for when a connector is what you needed — not a better prompt.

Part 04

What you're handing over

Connecting a service grants Claude access to read — and sometimes modify — data inside it. Four things worth understanding before you click authorise.

The one that reassures people most

Claude sees what you see

Claude can only reach data you already have access to. Connecting your work email doesn't hand Claude your CEO's inbox — only your own. A connector inherits your permissions; it doesn't expand them.

Rule 02

Scoped access

Permissions are specific to what the connector needs, and you can toggle individual permissions on and off inside each application's own menu.

Rule 03

Revocable at any time

Disconnect through Claude's settings, or from the third-party service's own security settings. Two doors out, and neither requires anyone's help.

Rule 04

Custom connectors, same caution

You can find or build your own, exactly as with Skills — and the warning carries across unchanged. Only install from sources you trust.

The habit: the permissions screen is the only moment you get to decide. Once you've authorised, you've authorised — so read that screen at the speed you'd read a contract, not the speed you'd read a cookie banner.
