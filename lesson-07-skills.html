Expertise
on a shelf

A Skill is a folder of instructions, scripts and resources that Claude picks up when the task calls for it. Projects hold what Claude knows. Skills hold how Claude works. You don't invoke them — they fire themselves.

Check this before you skip anything

The plan requirement has moved — and it was never really the plan

Course material on Skills often opens by saying it's a feature preview for Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise, and tells Free-plan readers to skip the hands-on parts. Anthropic's help centre now says otherwise.

  • Skills are available on Free, Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise. If you're on Free, don't skip this lesson — do it.
  • The real dependency is code execution, not your subscription. A greyed-out Skills menu is almost always this, and almost never the plan.
  • On Team and Enterprise an owner can switch it off for the whole organisation — so if the setting isn't in your own settings, that's who to ask.

Source: Anthropic Help Centre, "Use skills in Claude", checked August 2026. Product pages move faster than course pages — when the two disagree, believe the help centre.

The thing nobody tells you

You've been using Skills for months without knowing it.

Every time Claude made you an Excel sheet, a Word document, a PowerPoint deck or a PDF, a Skill was doing the work behind the scenes. This lesson isn't introducing a new feature — it's naming something you've already relied on, and then handing you the ability to build your own.

Kind 01

Anthropic's

Built and maintained by Anthropic, covering document creation for Excel, Word, PowerPoint and PDF. Nothing to configure — Claude invokes them when they're relevant.

Kind 02

Yours

Ones you or your organisation create for specialised workflows and domain-specific work. This is where the value is, because your methodology isn't in anyone's default.

  • Apply your company's brand guidelines to every presentation
  • Structure meeting notes in your exact format
  • Run your quarterly variance analysis the same way each quarter
  • Walk a compliance checklist without skipping a line

The definition worth keeping: a Skill codifies a repeatable workflow, so Claude follows the same rigorous steps every time — rather than improvising a slightly different process on each occasion.

Switching it on

Because Skills can contain executable code

Four rules for what you install

  • Only install custom Skills from sources you trust. A Skill isn't just text — it can carry scripts that run.
  • Anthropic's built-in Skills are tested and maintained by Anthropic. Those you can take as read.
  • Custom Skills you upload are private to your account. They don't leak sideways to other people.
  • Installing something from outside? Read it first. Open the contents and understand what it does before you switch it on.

The behaviour that surprises people

In Chat, your original file is never touched

Claude doesn't edit the document in place. It produces a new version and hands that back. Two consequences worth holding onto: your original is safe no matter what Claude does to it — and the improved version only exists if you download it. Close the tab without saving and the work is gone.

Part 04

Making your own

This is the part worth doing today. You don't write a Skill, and you don't touch a file structure — you get interviewed about one, and Claude builds it.

Step 01

Say what you want to make

Start a new chat and describe it plainly. "I want to create a skill for writing quarterly business reviews." "I need a skill that applies our brand guidelines to presentations."

Step 02

Answer the interview

Claude asks about your workflow rather than waiting for a spec. Expect questions like:

  • What should this skill do?
  • What makes output good for this kind of work?
  • When would you reach for it?

Answer these as if briefing a new colleague. The quality of the Skill is mostly the quality of your answers here.

Step 03

Hand over your reference material

Templates, style guides, brand assets — and above all, examples of work you're proud of. A finished piece you rate teaches the standard faster than any description of it.

Step 04

Save it

Claude generates a properly structured skill file. You save it. That's the whole technical step.

Step 05

Find it, and keep improving it

The Customize tab in the left sidebar lists every skill available to you. Edit them by hand, or just ask Claude to change one and it rewrites the files for you. From here on it fires automatically whenever the work is relevant — no manual triggering ever again.

Why this beats writing instructions yourself: you know your process but you've never had to articulate it. The interview drags out the steps you do without thinking — which are usually the ones that make your work yours.

Part 05

Skills or Projects?

Both give Claude more context, so the two get confused constantly. One sentence separates them for good.

Projects store knowledge.
Skills perform tasks.

Projects

Knowledge hubs — the what
Purpose
Store the knowledge Claude references.
Best for
Long-term context, reference materials, team collaboration.
Looks like
A customer hub, a research workspace, a feedback generator.
Persistence
Knowledge is available across every chat inside the project.

Skills

Procedural machines — the how
Purpose
Define the processes Claude executes.
Best for
Repeatable workflows, multi-step tasks, consistent methodology.
Looks like
Brand or legal guidelines, blog drafting, PDF creation.
Persistence
Instructions applied whenever the skill is invoked.
They're better together

A Skill can read from a Project. Your customer call prep skill pulls the customer profiles sitting in a project's knowledge base — the project supplies the information, the skill supplies the process. If you find yourself uploading the same procedure as a document, that's a Skill wearing a Project's clothes.
